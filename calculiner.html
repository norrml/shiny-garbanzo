CALCULINERv1.4

What Calculiner does (click to fold)
  • Live results. Type any expression and the answer appears greyed out on the same line (=8). Spacing around operators doesn't matter. Each Enter-line is its own calculation, and rows are shaded alternately. Long lines use the full box width before wrapping.
  • Colours. Green = a date word, date term, day/week/month/year unit, calendar function or date result the app recognised. Blue = a locked number result. Red = a variable (its definition and wherever you use it). Grey = live results, suggestions and notes. Light grey = text the app detected and ignored (comments). Strikethrough = a calculation you left unlocked and moved on from.
  • Keyboard. Enter moves the cursor to the end of the next line; it never adds a line, and does nothing on the last line (or when there is only one line). Shift+Enter adds a new line and moves the cursor onto it; if the line has a live result it locks it first (like =). Pressed in a comment, on a text-only line or in the middle of a line, it is a plain new line. Tab = accept a suggestion, or lock the result when no suggestion is showing. → also accepts a suggestion. Lines are only removed on purpose: Backspace and Delete only ever remove characters. They never join or remove a line, even when your selection runs across several lines (the selected text is cleared and the lines stay). Use Shift+Backspace (or Shift+Delete) to remove a line break, or a selection together with its line breaks. Cut (Ctrl+X) and typing over a multi-line selection follow the same rule. These shortcuts are for a physical keyboard; phone keyboards can't do them reliably.
  • Operators. + - * / ÷ ( ) %. Multiplication is * only (the × sign is not supported). Percent is relative: 200+10% = 220, 200-10% = 180, and a lone 50% = 0.5.
  • Factorials. Off by default. Tick "Factorials (!)" to allow 5! (whole numbers 0 to 170). While off, a line containing 5! shows no result and a grey note explains why. Factorials work with separators, locking and variables like any other calculation.
  • Several calculations on one line. A ; starts a new calculation straight away. A space also starts a new one when a number, bracket or date follows a finished number, bracket or date with no operator between them (30+4 2+3, today tomorrow). Spaces around operators (3 + 4) change nothing, and 2 v with a variable still multiplies. Only the last calculation on a line shows the live grey result and can be locked. Any earlier calculation you didn't lock with =, Tab or Shift+Enter is treated as ignored and shown struck through, with no result. Bare numbers are never struck (5 6 is just two numbers). After a locked result, a space followed by a new calculation starts a new one (3+4=7 5*2); a space followed by an operator continues the chain (3+4=7 +5); to begin a new calculation with a minus sign, use ;. A trailing space keeps the grey result of what you just typed (30+4 still shows =34).
  • Comments. A # anywhere starts a comment that runs to the end of the line, shown light grey and ignored. A line containing a # shows no live grey result (locking with = before the # still works). Words the app ignores inside a calculation (like rent in rent 500 + 200), words with digits (v99t) and ignored variable names are light grey too. Inside a comment, Tab does nothing, Shift+Enter makes a plain new line (no lock), and = is just a character.
  • Dates. Use today, tomorrow, yesterday, weekday names, or a date with a month name: 26 oct, 26 oct 2026, oct 26, 26th October (no year = this year). Weekdays: friday = the first Friday after today; next friday = Friday of next week (weeks run Monday to Sunday); last friday = the most recent Friday before today. Add or subtract whole days, weeks, months, years: today + 2 weeks, tomorrow - 3 days, next friday + 1 month. Two dates subtracted give the days between. A weekday written next to a full date (Fri 6 Nov) is just a label. Month maths stops at month end (31 Jan + 1 month = 28 Feb). Numeric dates like 26/10/2026 are not supported. Dates can't be stored in variables. Holidays, working days and business days are not handled.
  • Dash terms. A date or unit joined with dashes is read as one term: 20-march-2026, 3-weeks, 1-day. You can type them yourself, or let autocomplete do it: type 20 marc and the grey ghost shows h-2026; Tab turns the whole thing into 20-march-2026 (the current year is filled in and then pinned into the text). Likewise 3 we + Tab gives 3-weeks. A dash with spaces around it (20 - 3) is still a minus, and 20-3 is still 17. A date typed without a year (20 march) keeps meaning the current year, so it will change on 1 January; use Tab to pin the year. Words like days, day, weeks, months, years can't be variable names.
  • Calendar functions. Convert between days, weeks, months and years with the exact words days-to-weeks, days-to-months, days-to-years, weeks-to-days, weeks-to-months, and so on (all 12 pairs, singular or plural; no spaces around the dashes). Days are the smallest unit and results are whole numbers: 10 days-to-weeks = 1 week 3 days. After a date subtraction (typed directly or locked, e.g. 27 mar 2027 - today days-to-months) the answer is calendar-exact: whole years, then whole months from the earlier date (stopping at month end, 31 Jan + 1 month = 28 Feb), then leftover days. In that case the function must start with days-to-. After a locked day count the word "days" is shared with the function: type -to-weeks straight after =-63 days, or a space and the whole days-to-weeks; either way the line reads =-63 days-to-weeks with "days" appearing once (an older doubled =-63 days days-to-weeks repairs itself). After a plain number (150 days-to-months) it uses a rough rule: week = 7 days, month = 30 days, year = 360 days. Zero units are left out; a negative span shows a minus on the front. The result is text and can't be used in further maths. Calendar-exact is a counting convention (the start day is not included), not legal advice: courts and contracts may count differently.
  • Leaving the page open. The app checks the date every 30 seconds, and when you return to the tab. If the day changes, results that use today, tomorrow, yesterday or weekday names are recalculated automatically (locked ones too) and a banner tells you. Dates you typed out in full never change. The toolbar shows the date the app is using.
  • Autocomplete. When you type 2+ letters at the end of a line, the rest appears in grey: today, tomorrow, yesterday, weekdays, months, and your own variable names (date words are checked first). After a whole number it suggests days, weeks, months, years first (mo becomes months, not Monday) and then month names, and accepting rewrites the term with dashes. Inside a calendar function it completes the second unit (days-to-we suggests eks). Press Tab or → to accept. After a locked result and a space you're starting a new calculation, so normal suggestions apply. No suggestions inside a comment. While a suggestion shows, the result on that line is hidden.
  • Unfinished input. 39+1+ still shows =40, and open brackets are closed for you.
  • Locking a result. Type =, press Tab (when no suggestion is showing), or press Shift+Enter. The last calculation's answer turns blue (green for dates and day counts). = and Tab are ignored when there is nothing new to lock (right after a result, on an empty line, or right after ;), so you can't duplicate a result. Keep typing an operator after a locked result to continue from it (today= then + 3 days). A locked day count can be followed by a calendar function (27 mar 2027 - today=148 days-to-months); the word "days" is shared, see Calendar functions. Edit anything earlier and the locked results update. Backspace removes the = and its result together. A digit can't be typed straight after a locked result; type a space or an operator first. Don't type the words days, weeks, months or years right after a locked number (e.g. =5 weeks), except as the start of a calendar function like days-to-weeks: otherwise the app reads them as part of the result.
  • Variables. Type name=number (e.g. v=1.5) and it turns red; later uses of the name are red too. Names are letters only, not case-sensitive, and whole-word: v and vv are different variables. Several can share a line: v=1 rr=2 dd=4 or v=1 ; rr=2. Variables work on every line, above or below where they're defined. 2v multiplies (=3), 1+v = 2.5, and today + n days works. If a name is defined twice, the lowest definition wins. Names containing digits (v99t=3) are treated as comments. A definition must come first in its line or after a ;, and a variable definition is never struck through.
  • Reserved words can't be variables. A variable with one of these names is ignored: a grey note appears on the line and it shows dimmed in the variable list. (Typing today= or sun= locks the date instead, since = means lock.) Reserved:
  • Variable list. Every detected variable and its value is shown under the box. A name defined more than once is flagged in red with the line that wins. Ignored names appear dimmed.
  • Saving. Everything you type and your settings are saved automatically in this browser (the toolbar shows when). It is not a backup: clearing browser data, a private window, or moving the file (in some browsers) loses it. Clear deletes the sheet and its saved copy after asking.
  • Popup window. "Open in popup" opens Calculiner in its own small window. It can't stay on top of other windows (browsers don't allow that). The popup and the main window share the same saved sheet and stay in sync; type in one at a time.
  • Comment words in calculations. Letters, commas and other punctuation are skipped, so rent 500 + food 200 gives 700. Careful: a comment word that matches a defined variable is read as that variable. A weekday word (sat, sun, wed, mon ...) anywhere is read as a date, and a month word next to a day number (mar 5, may-5) is too. A 4-digit number right after a day and month is read as the year.
  • Rounding. Results are rounded to 2 decimals by default (choose 0 to 5). Each locked result carries forward exactly as shown.
  • Commas in results. Off by default. Choose International (1,234,567), Indian (12,34,567) or East Asian (123,4567).
  • Safeguards. Division by zero, overflow, invalid factorials, impossible dates (31 Feb), non-whole conversions and malformed lines show no result, so you never see a wrong number.
